Study notes & exercises+
The idea

Start with an intention, not a frame count. Tethered shooting lets everyone see light placement, expression and small changes as they happen.

What to watch

Check who controls the camera, who directs the model, where images appear, and how pairs rotate. Agree on model comfort, safe cable routes and who may move lights.

Your exercise

Write one lighting question you want answered today.

09:15–09:45
Study notes & exercises+
The idea

Classic Hollywood portrait lighting uses deliberate direction and shadow shape. A raised, angled key can define the cheekbones; fill or bounce controls how dark the shadows become.

What to watch

Watch the catchlights and nose shadow. Note key height, angle, distance, modifier and background separation. Compare the face with and without fill.

Your exercise

Before a light is moved, predict how the nose shadow and cheek highlight will change.

Further reference: Workbook: First model / reference lighting sketch.Open workbook ↗
09:45–11:45
Study notes & exercises+
The idea

A strong portrait balances the model’s expression, the shape of light and the pose. A beautiful gown also needs enough exposure and direction to show its texture.

What to watch

Study catchlights, chin and jaw shadows, hand placement, fabric folds, hair separation and whether the background distracts.

Your exercise

Make two deliberate frames: a close Hollywood headshot and a wider gown portrait. Change one thing between frames and compare.

Further reference: Workbook: Classic Hollywood; Watch, predict, compare.Open workbook ↗
11:45–12:00
Study notes & exercises+
The idea

Review before resetting. The most useful lesson may be the adjustment that improved a frame, not the final exposure itself.

What to watch

Identify the key light position and one successful lighting change. Record your exposure and flash or LED settings before the setup disappears.

Your exercise

Choose your strongest first-look image and explain why its light supports the subject.

12:45–13:15
Study notes & exercises+
The idea

Coloured LEDs, neutral flash and reflective fabric interact. Colour separation comes from source angle and balance, not simply using more saturation.

What to watch

Notice where each coloured source hits skin, hair, fabric and backdrop. Watch for unwanted colour spill, flare and clipped highlights.

Your exercise

Predict what changing a single LED hue or moving a rim light will do before Gareth demonstrates it.

Further reference: Workbook: Second model / colour-light reference sketch.Open workbook ↗
13:15–15:15
Study notes & exercises+
The idea

Contemporary portraiture can use light, fabric and movement as graphic elements. Consistent skin rendition and a clear visual intention help bold colour feel purposeful.

What to watch

Track fabric movement, rim-light separation, face orientation, shutter/flash effects and how reflection changes when the model turns.

Your exercise

Make a luminous colour portrait and an experimental editorial frame. Vary one source, pose or movement at a time.

Further reference: Workbook: Contemporary colour & shine; Make one change.Open workbook ↗
15:15–16:00
Study notes & exercises+
The idea

Edit for intention rather than quantity. Discuss whether the eye goes where the photographer wanted, and which lighting decision contributed most.

What to watch

Evaluate expression, shadow shape, catchlights, background, colour relationships and whether wardrobe and pose support the image.

Your exercise

Select four images: Hollywood headshot, gown portrait, colour portrait, editorial experiment. Name one strength and one improvement for each.
